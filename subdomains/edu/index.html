Physics you can poke.

Small interactive tools I use when I teach physics and quantum computing. Each one shows a single idea, runs in a browser, and works on a phone.

Tools

  • A qubit you can turn

    Apply quantum gates to one qubit, watch it move on the Bloch sphere, then measure it a hundred times.

    quantum computing · class 11 and up

  • Double slit, one particle at a time

    Send particles through two slits and watch the pattern build up dot by dot. Then put a detector at the slits.

    waves and quantum · class 12 and up

  • A pendulum, timed

    Drag the bob and let go. Compare the measured period with 2π√(L/g), on Earth, the Moon or Jupiter.

    mechanics · class 9 and up

  • Standing waves on a string

    Change the harmonic, tension and length. See the nodes, then hear the note the string would play.

    waves and sound · class 11 and up

  • Projectile, with and without air

    Launch at any angle and speed. Find the angle for the longest throw, then switch on air resistance.

    mechanics · class 9 and up

  • Photoelectric effect

    Shine light of different colours on a metal and find the work function.

    being drawn

  • Spectra of atoms

    Why hydrogen glows in four colours, from the energy levels.

    being drawn

For teachers

Use any of these in class, on a projector or on students' phones. There is nothing to install and no sign-up. Each tool has a few "try this" prompts and a short note on the physics behind it, so you can set it as a small activity.

The numbers are real: the pendulum swings by the full equation, not the small-angle shortcut, and the double slit uses the actual interference formula. Where a tool simplifies something, it says so.

If something is wrong, unclear, or you'd like a tool for a topic you teach, tell me.

Why this exists

I taught physics at two colleges in Kerala and later wrote curricula for school students. The moment a concept lands is usually when a student gets to change something and see what happens. These tools are my attempt to give them that, one idea at a time.

More about me at ajmal.science. My research lives at research.ajmal.science, and work in progress at labs.ajmal.science.
