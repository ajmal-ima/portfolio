Double slit, one particle at a time

Particles go through two narrow slits, one at a time, and land on a screen. Watch what pattern they build.

0 particles

Try this

  1. Fire 1, then 10. One particle lands in one place. Nothing about a single dot looks like a wave.
  2. Fire 1000. The stripes appear out of single dots. Each particle went through the setup alone.
  3. Make the slits closer together (smaller d). Do the fringes spread out or squeeze in? Check with Δy = λL/d.
  4. Change the colour from violet to red. Which colour spreads more?
  5. Tick put a detector at the slits and fire 1000. Knowing which slit each particle used wipes out the stripes.
the physics ✎

Each slit acts as a source. On a far screen the two paths differ by d·sinθ. Where that difference is a whole number of wavelengths, the waves add up; where it is a half number, they cancel.

I(θ) ∝ cos²(πd sinθ / λ) · [sin(β)/β]², β = πa sinθ / λ

The cos² part is the two-slit interference. The second part is the envelope from each slit having a width a. For small angles the bright fringes are Δy = λL/d apart.

The pattern is a probability: it tells you where each particle is likely to land, not where any one will. If you find out which slit it went through, the two paths no longer interfere and you get two overlapping single-slit humps. (Here that is drawn as the envelope alone, which is a simplification.)
