A pendulum, timed

Drag the bob out and let it go. The tool times each swing and compares it with the formula from the textbook.

where are you?

start angle

Try this

  1. Start at 5°. The measured period matches 2π√(L/g) almost exactly.
  2. Start at 60°, then 120°. The formula and the swing start to disagree. Which one is wrong, and why?
  3. Make the pendulum four times longer. What happens to the period? (Not four times.)
  4. Take it to the Moon. How long would a 1 m pendulum take for one swing there?
  5. Add some air friction. The swing dies down, but does the period change much?
the physics ✎

The bob feels gravity pulling it back towards the bottom. Along the arc, the restoring force is mg sinθ, which gives the equation of motion

d²θ/dt² = −(g/L) sinθ

For small angles, sinθ ≈ θ and the motion is simple harmonic with period T = 2π√(L/g). The mass doesn't appear at all.

This tool solves the full equation step by step (fourth-order Runge–Kutta), so at large angles you see the real period, which is longer than the formula. At 60° it is about 7% longer.
