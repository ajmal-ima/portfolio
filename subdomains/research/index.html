Molecules, computed.

I use quantum mechanics to study molecules: how they're shaped, how they vibrate, and what that says about how they behave. This page collects that work, along with research I do at QuantumX and earlier projects.

PhD

Molecular quantum mechanics of bioactive compounds

Kalasalingam Academy of Research and Education · part-time · since Dec 2022
supervised by Dr. P. Selvarangan

Computational and spectroscopic studies of bioactive compounds, with a focus on molecules relevant to cancer research.

How a study goes

  1. Pick a molecule. A compound with known or suspected biological activity.
  2. Compute it. Quantum-mechanical calculations give its most stable shape, how its electrons are spread out, and the frequencies it should vibrate at.
  3. Measure it. Spectra from the lab show which frequencies it actually absorbs.
  4. Compare. When calculation and spectrum agree, we can trust the model, and use it to say things the experiment alone can't, such as which parts of the molecule are most reactive.

There's a small sketch of an infrared spectrum on my main site if you'd like to see what one looks like.
